# Terms of Use

> **Template — review before you rely on it.** Like the privacy notice, this is
> a working draft written for how DuoCount is actually built. It is **not legal
> advice**; have a professional adapt it (especially the governing-law section)
> before offering the service commercially.

_Last updated: July 2026_

These terms are an agreement between the business that creates a DuoCount store
(the **store**, "you") and the operator of this DuoCount deployment (the
**operator**, "we"). Creating a store on the sign-up screen is how you accept
them.

## 1. What DuoCount is

DuoCount is a store-operations tool: signed cash-drawer, scratch-off, and
inventory counts; shift notes and incident write-ups; time-clock and schedules;
reports; and an optional customer-rewards program. Records are designed to be
**append-only and signed** — that tamper-evidence is the product, not a
limitation.

## 2. Your account and your staff

- The person who creates the store is its **owner** and is responsible for the
  store's use of DuoCount — including who they invite, the roles they assign,
  and keeping PINs private.
- Staff accounts belong to the store. Add only people who work for you, use
  real names your team recognizes, and deactivate people who leave.
- You are responsible for complying with the laws that apply to *your*
  business — employment records, lottery rules, and consumer-protection laws
  where you operate.

## 3. Your data is yours

- **Everything your store records belongs to your store**: counts, notes,
  incidents, punches, settings — and, if you enable rewards, **your customer
  list and its points ledger**.
- We act as a **service provider** (a "processor" in privacy-law terms): we
  store and process your data to run the service for you, and for nothing else.
- **We never pool or share your customers across stores**, never market to
  your customers, and never sell anyone's data. (Some loyalty networks share
  customer identities between merchants — DuoCount deliberately does not.)
- You can export your operational records as CSV from the app at any time. If
  you close your store, ask the operator for a final export; store data is
  deleted within **90 days** of a closure request, except records we must keep
  for legal or security reasons.

## 4. If you turn on customer rewards

The rewards program creates duties that are **yours as the merchant**:

- **Tell customers the deal.** Post the printable counter sign (Admin → Reward
  settings) and make the program's terms available — several US states (e.g.
  California's CCPA) require a **notice of financial incentive** describing
  what data you collect (a phone number and optional name), what the points
  are worth, and how a customer can leave the program. A ready-to-adapt notice
  is included in the [Privacy & Data Handling](/docs/privacy-and-data) page.
- **Only enroll people who ask to join.** The phone number a customer gives you
  at the register is for the points program — nothing else.
- **No text-message marketing through DuoCount.** DuoCount does not send SMS,
  and you must not use exported customer phone numbers for texting campaigns
  unless you have separately collected the express written consent US law
  (TCPA) requires. Text-message lawsuits routinely reach small businesses.
- **Honor requests.** If a customer asks to leave the program or have their
  data removed, stop using their number and ask the operator to delete the
  record; do not re-enroll them without being asked.
- Excluded categories (tobacco, vape, alcohol, lottery, gift cards, fuel) stay
  excluded from earning and redemption — discount bans and minimum-price laws
  apply to them.

## 5. Acceptable use

Don't use DuoCount to break the law, to store data you have no right to
collect, to probe or overload the service, or to mislead your staff (for
example, by editing the record — which the design prevents — or by
misrepresenting what the audit trail shows).

## 6. The service, warranties, and liability

DuoCount is provided **as-is**. It is a record-keeping aid, not an accounting,
payroll, tax, or legal system, and its alerts are **questions, not verdicts** —
a variance flag is a prompt for a conversation, not an accusation. See the
[legal disclaimers](/docs/legal-disclaimers) page, which is part of these
terms. To the extent the law allows, the operator's total liability for any
claim related to the service is limited to the amount you paid for it in the
twelve months before the claim.

## 7. Ending things

You can stop using DuoCount at any time and take your data with you (section
3). The operator may suspend a store that violates these terms — where
practical, with notice and a chance to export first.

## 8. Changes

If these terms change in a way that matters, the operator will make the new
version available in the app's documentation before it takes effect.
